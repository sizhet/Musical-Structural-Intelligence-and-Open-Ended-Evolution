# Glossary — Musical Structural Intelligence and Open-Ended Evolution (MSI-OEE)

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Authors:** Sizhe Tan & GPT-Obot  
**Repository:** `Musical-Structural-Intelligence-and-Open-Ended-Evolution`  
**Status:** Research terminology for a conceptual framework; definitions are operational proposals, not claims of established scientific consensus.

## Purpose and conventions

This glossary provides a common vocabulary for the eight MSI research papers, three cases, four specifications, and validation documents. It distinguishes **observed phenomena**, **proposed representations**, **testable hypotheses**, and **long-term possibilities**. A term's presence here does not imply that its proposed mechanism has been empirically demonstrated.

- **Musical** denotes sound, symbolic music, performance, or musically meaningful temporal organization, as specified by a study.
- **Subject** denotes an explicitly modeled evaluation or interaction perspective. In AI experiments, this need not imply consciousness or subjective experience.
- **Identity**, **similarity**, **function**, **preference**, and **value** are distinct constructs; none should be silently substituted for another.
- **OTHERS** is a register for currently unexplained evidence, not an arbitrary residual bonus or proof of hidden intelligence.

## A — C

### Acoustic perception
The sensing and computational discrimination of audio properties such as pitch, onset, duration, intensity, timbre, and spatial cues. Acoustic recognition alone does not establish subjective musical experience. See **Musical understanding** and **Subjective musical experience**.

### Aesthetic alignment
The adaptation of a musical system to a specified evaluator's expressed or behaviorally inferred preferences. Alignment should be distinguished from creative achievement and from claims of universal aesthetic value. See **Scoring sovereignty** and **Bidirectional musical relay**.

### Aesthetic translation
A structured transformation or explanatory sequence intended to make a musical organization more accessible to a different audience or evaluator while documenting what is preserved and changed. Translation success must be evaluated, not assumed. See **Intermediate Musical UIERU**.

### AI-native music
A proposed research category for musical structures explored or selected primarily by AI-based generation and evaluation systems, rather than being designed solely to reproduce established human genres. The label does not imply that AI experiences enjoyment or that the resulting structures are automatically artistically valuable.

### AI-native musical signature
A repeatable and distinguishable distribution of musical choices, structures, transformations, or compositional policies associated with an AI system. Signature distinctiveness must be tested against training-data memorization, prompt effects, and genre conventions.

### Anti-Goodhart governance
Methods that prevent optimization of an imperfect scoring proxy from being mistaken for genuine musical improvement. Examples include independent evaluators, held-out listeners, adversarial tests, counter-evidence, and reporting failures. See **Evaluator governance**.

### Bidirectional musical relay
A two-way exchange in which human and AI participants communicate musical structures, judgments, and revisions; both sides may adapt, but neither must surrender its evaluation authority. This is a proposed interaction architecture, not evidence of equal subjective experience.

### Boundary intelligence
The capacity to identify, represent, and revise the boundaries of a candidate musical unit according to structure, context, task, and perspective. Boundaries may be nested, overlapping, or uncertain.

### Candidate Musical UIERU
A musical pattern proposed as a reusable, function-bearing structural unit whose identity, transformation behavior, and relay interfaces require validation. Recurrence alone does not establish UIERU status.

### Collective musical learning
Accumulation and transmission of musical practices, patterns, techniques, and evaluation conventions across people, systems, or generations. It may occur without a centralized learner or a single shared aesthetic objective.

### Composition as structural relay
The view that musical composition involves selecting, transforming, coordinating, and developing relationships among units—not merely concatenating musical fragments. See **Relay graph**.

### Contextual similarity
Resemblance assessed with reference to genre, musical role, surrounding phrases, performance conditions, or listening task. It can differ from note-level or audio-level similarity.

### Counter-evidence
Observations, examples, or analyses that challenge a proposed musical identity, similarity explanation, transformation rule, or scoring hypothesis. Counter-evidence should remain traceable even when a candidate is revised or rejected.

### Creative deviation
An intentional or systematically selected departure from a musical expectation, pattern, timing convention, or compositional rule that may acquire expressive value under a particular context and perspective. Not every deviation is creative or valuable.

### Cross-subject aesthetic translation intelligence
A proposed capability to identify and construct interpretable bridges between different musical perspectives, including human audiences and AI evaluation systems. It should be tested through learning, comprehension, and structural-preservation outcomes rather than through self-reported model success.

## D — H

### Dynamic unit boundary
A musical unit boundary whose location or confidence varies with musical context, listening task, transformation, or evaluator. Dynamic does not mean unconstrained; competing segmentations should be compared empirically.

### Embodied musical intelligence
Musical perception, production, or evaluation shaped by bodily action and sensorimotor constraints, such as breath, gesture, movement, timing coordination, and performance feedback. An AI may possess functional sensorimotor loops without thereby having human embodiment or experience.

### Emergent musical value
Value attributed to relationships or large-scale organization that cannot be adequately predicted from isolated unit scores alone. The claim of emergence should be tested against appropriate additive and interaction-based baselines.

### Evaluator governance
The explicit management of who or what evaluates music, with which data, criteria, authority, uncertainty, and revision process. See **Anti-Goodhart governance** and **Scoring sovereignty**.

### Evidence provenance
The traceable origin of a musical observation or claim: audio recording, symbolic score, human judgment, expert annotation, model prediction, historical source, or experimental intervention. Provenance is essential when interpreting **OTHERS** and possible copying relationships.

### Expressive deviation
A departure in timing, articulation, pitch, dynamics, phrasing, or another musical dimension that listeners or performers may interpret as meaningful expression. Its interpretation depends on context and perspective.

### Functional identity
Continuity of a musical unit's role—such as cadence, transition, tension building, or thematic recall—under a specified interpretation. Functional identity may change even when structural identity remains stable, and vice versa.

### Functional preference (AI)
A stable operational tendency of an AI system to rank, select, or optimize certain musical structures. It is not evidence that the system consciously likes or enjoys music.

### Human-grounded scoring
Evaluation informed by actual human judgments or behaviors under documented conditions. Human-grounded does not mean universally objective: listeners and cultures can disagree, and preference can change over time.

### Human–AI musical co-evolution
A proposed process in which AI musical exploration, human learning, feedback, compositional practice, and evaluation criteria change through repeated interaction. Evidence for co-evolution requires tracking changes on both sides rather than only improved AI imitation of existing human preferences.

## I — M

### Identity-preserving transformation
A musical transformation that retains a declared aspect of identity under specified conditions and evidence. Preservation is conditional, not universal. See **Structural invariant** and **Transformation contract**.

### Intermediate Musical UIERU
A bridging musical unit or representation used to connect unfamiliar structures with more accessible ones during aesthetic translation. It should document which features of the source remain recognizable or functionally relevant.

### Localization (musical)
Selecting relevant musical units, models, transformations, or evaluators for a given compositional or analytical context. In MSI-OEE, localization precedes or guides per-unit processing and relay.

### Metric distance
A mathematical distance satisfying non-negativity, identity of indiscernibles, symmetry, and the triangle inequality. Many useful musical dissimilarity or human-judgment scores do **not** satisfy all these axioms and should not be called metrics without verification.

### Motif
A short musical idea recognizable through some combination of melodic contour, rhythm, harmony, timbre, articulation, or contextual function. A motif is a candidate Musical UIERU, not automatically a validated one.

### Multi-perspective Scoring Forest
A structured collection of evaluation dimensions, models, or operators associated with potentially different subjects and contexts. It can preserve incompatible judgments rather than forcing them into one aggregate score.

### Musical distance
A task-dependent measure of difference between musical objects. It may use audio features, symbolic patterns, learned embeddings, transformation-aware structure, or human judgments; its mathematical properties must be stated.

### Musical identity
A specified continuity relation between musical objects under transformations, contexts, or perspectives. Identity must be operationalized—for example, same recorded performance, same motif family, or same musical role—rather than treated as one universal binary fact.

### Musical information representation (IR)
A representation of musical information in sound, notation, symbolic events, learned embeddings, structured graphs, performance gestures, or other media. No single representation is assumed sufficient for all musical tasks.

### Musical Relay
The transfer, transformation, combination, or reinterpretation of musical units across compositional stages, representations, performers, systems, or subjects. Relay may change form while retaining selected structural or functional relations.

### Musical signature
A statistically or structurally identifiable pattern of choices across a motif, work, composer, performer, style, or system. A signature is not by itself proof of authorship or originality.

### Musical structural intelligence
The proposed ability to identify, manipulate, evaluate, explain, and grow reusable musical structures across tasks and contexts. The MSI-OEE framework treats this as a research program requiring empirical tests.

### Musical UIERU
A proposed **Universal Intelligence Evolution and Relay Unit** instantiated in music: a context-sensitive, reusable, function-bearing structural unit with declared identity conditions, transformation behavior, relay interfaces, and evidence status. Its exact granularity and empirical validity remain open research questions.

### Musical understanding
Task-specific ability to recognize or infer musical organization, relations, expectations, and functions. Successful analysis or prediction does not by itself establish human-like emotional experience.

### Musical worldview
An evolving configuration of musical expectations, learned categories, memories, interpretive habits, and valuation criteria associated with a subject or community. It is a modeling construct, not necessarily a complete psychological theory.

## N — R

### Open evidence
A research approach that preserves observations unexplained by existing features and models, investigates alternative hypotheses, and admits validated new explanatory dimensions. See **OTHERS**.

### Open-ended musical evolution
The hypothesized ongoing emergence of new musical units, transformations, compositional strategies, and possibly evaluation dimensions. High output volume or stylistic variation alone does not demonstrate open-ended evolution.

### OTHERS
An explicitly tracked space of unresolved musical evidence, residuals, or disagreement that existing representations do not adequately explain. Candidate categories include structural, perceptual, contextual, perspective, provenance, translation, and evaluation residuals. An OTHERS entry is a question for investigation, not a confirmed hidden feature.

### OTHERS evidence register
A provenance-aware record of unexplained observations, current baselines, candidate explanations, uncertainties, counter-evidence, and validation status. See [SPEC-004](specs/SPEC-004-OTHERS-Evidence-and-Validation.md).

### Perceptual similarity
Resemblance judged by human listeners or modeled from perceptual data under a defined task. It may depend on training, culture, attention, memory, and presentation conditions.

### Perspective
The situated standpoint from which a subject or system perceives, interprets, evaluates, or acts upon music. A perspective should specify the task, context, and evidence supporting attributed preferences.

### Perspective-conditioned scoring
Evaluation of music under an explicitly specified subject, context, task, and scoring model. Different perspectives may legitimately yield different results.

### Perspective distance
A proposed comparison of how two evaluators or contexts represent and value musical objects. It should not be confused with a literal spatial metric unless the required mathematical properties are demonstrated.

### Provenance similarity
Evidence relevant to possible historical connection, access, transmission, or derivation between musical works. It is distinct from acoustic or structural resemblance; resemblance alone cannot establish provenance.

### Relay graph
A representation of musical units and their compositional dependencies, transformations, timing relations, and interactions as nodes and edges. A relay graph can support analysis, generation, revision, and traceability.

### Romantic Intelligence
An MSI-OEE **proposed research construct** for generating, recognizing, and evaluating meaningful creative deviations and for exploring value not fully captured by existing objectives. It is not synonymous with randomness, imprecision, sentimentality, or proven artificial emotion.

### Romantic Plane
A conceptual evaluation perspective emphasizing expressive freedom, ambiguity, novelty, and the possibility of changing value criteria. It complements rather than replaces a **Precision Plane**.

## S — Z

### Scoring Forest
An organized collection of scoring operators or criteria that may evaluate structure, perception, emotion, cultural fit, originality, function, or other dimensions. Scores are conditioned by their definitions and data; they do not constitute objective aesthetic truth.

### Scoring sovereignty
The principle that an evaluation perspective retains an explicit owner or authority and is not silently overwritten by another subject's scoring model. For AI systems, this is a governance and modeling convention, not a declaration of moral personhood.

### Soft musical contract
A compositional or relay interface specifying expected relations and permitted deviations, with contextual tolerances rather than rigid equality. Softness does not eliminate the need to state constraints and evaluate violations.

### Structural identity
Continuity of a declared musical organization—such as interval relations, rhythmic ratios, phrase topology, or transformation family—despite surface changes. Structural identity may be probabilistic or perspective-conditioned.

### Structural invariant
A property hypothesized to remain stable under a specified set of transformations, such as interval contour under transposition. An invariant must identify its transformation domain and failure conditions.

### Structural growth
The accumulation of independently validated, reusable musical capabilities—such as a new unit class, transformation operator, or scoring feature—that improve performance beyond existing baselines. More generated songs do not necessarily imply structural growth.

### Structural RSI (musical)
A proposed recursive structural self-improvement process in which an AI system suggests new musical units, operators, or evaluators, then subjects them to external testing and governance before promotion. Recursive self-scoring alone is insufficient.

### Subject boundary
The distinction between the experiences, information, action capacities, and evaluation authority of different musical participants. It is important when modeling composers, performers, listeners, communities, and AI systems.

### Subjective musical experience
First-person experience associated with listening, performing, remembering, or feeling music. Whether an AI system has such experience is unresolved; behavioral and computational competence should not be equated with phenomenology.

### Surface equality
Exact or near-exact correspondence of an explicitly chosen representation, such as MIDI notes, timing, or waveform segments. Surface equality is narrower than structural or functional identity.

### Transformation contract
A declared operator interface specifying inputs, permitted changes, intended invariants, output constraints, context, and evidence. See [SPEC-002](specs/SPEC-002-Transformation-and-Relay-Operators.md).

### Transformative relay
Relay in which a musical unit changes representation or organization while preserving or intentionally revising specified relations, functions, or identities.

### Two-Way CCC
A bidirectional consistency, challenge, and correction process in which musical interpretations or evaluations can be questioned and revised across participants or system layers. In MSI-OEE it is a proposed governance and learning mechanism, not a guarantee of consensus.

### UIERU
**Universal Intelligence Evolution and Relay Unit**: the broader structural-intelligence concept motivating the musical specialization in this repository. Musical UIERUs are candidate domain instances, not proof that one universal unit ontology has been established.

### Validation-in-scope
An evidence status indicating that a claim passed specified tests under declared data, transformations, tasks, populations, and conditions. It must not be generalized beyond those boundaries without further validation.

### Value discovery
The proposed identification of musical qualities or evaluative dimensions not adequately captured by a system's current objectives. Genuine discovery requires independent evidence beyond the system's own optimized score.

## Essential distinctions

| Do not conflate | With | Why the distinction matters |
|---|---|---|
| Recurring motif | Validated Musical UIERU | Reuse, function, boundaries, and relay require evidence. |
| Surface equality | Structural identity | Transformations can change notes or timbre while retaining relations. |
| Structural identity | Functional identity | The same motif may play different roles in different contexts. |
| Musical similarity | Historical copying | Resemblance does not establish source relationships. |
| Musical similarity | Copyright infringement | Legal findings depend on applicable law and evidence beyond a score. |
| Human preference prediction | Human subjective experience | Prediction is not first-person feeling. |
| AI functional preference | AI enjoyment | Stable selection behavior does not establish consciousness. |
| Novelty | Artistic value | Unusual outputs can be uninteresting or incoherent. |
| Generation volume | Structural growth | Growth requires validated transferable capability. |
| Model–human disagreement | New discovered feature | Disagreement may arise from noise, bias, or task mismatch. |
| OTHERS residual | Confirmed explanatory factor | Residuals require hypotheses, controls, and counter-evidence. |
| Scoring improvement | Cultural significance | Historical impact requires evidence across people and time. |

## Research integrity note

These definitions support clear discussion and falsifiable experimentation. They do not establish a complete theory of music, prove that music is reducible to UIERUs, establish AI sentience, or replace expert musical analysis. Claims should be accompanied by operational definitions, data provenance, suitable baselines, uncertainty, and potential falsifiers.
